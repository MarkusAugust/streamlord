## What it costs

Nothing.

The reason is not a promise, it is the licence. Streamlord is released under MIT, and **every
released version stays MIT**. That permission is irrevocable. It cannot be withdrawn by anyone,
including me, and it holds even if the project is one day set aside.

So you do not have to trust my intentions. You can read the licence file.

## What you may do

Everything ordinary, without asking:

- use Streamlord commercially, in as many projects as you like
- change it, and keep those changes to yourself
- ship it inside a product you sell, including a closed one
- publish your own version of it

The one condition is that the copyright notice and the licence text travel with copies of the
software. It is in the jar, so in practice this happens by itself.

You owe no further credit, no link back, and no notice that you are using it.

## What is not promised

MIT disclaims all warranty, and that is not a formality.

- no guarantee that anything works
- no obligation on me to answer, to fix, or to release again
- no timeline

Streamlord follows semantic versioning and the [changelog](/changelog/) records what a consumer
would notice, but a version contract is not a support contract. The licence makes the code yours
forever. It does not make me your supplier.

## The name is not included

MIT gives away the code. It does not give away the name.

**Streamlord**, **Gorvek**, **Gallowmark** and the mark are not part of the grant. Fork the code
and do what you like with it, but do not call the result Streamlord, publish a package under that
name, or suggest that it comes from here.

This is the usual arrangement in open source, and it exists for your sake as much as mine: a name
that means one particular thing is what makes it possible to say what you actually depend on.

## Contributions

**Issues are always welcome.** A bug report, a question or an idea costs you nothing to send and
me nothing to read.

**Pull requests are taken after a conversation.** Say what you want to do in an issue before you
write the code. That is not a barrier, it is a kindness: this repository enforces things that are
not obvious from the outside, and I would rather tell you before you spend an evening than after.

Whoever contributes signs the
[contributor agreement](https://github.com/MarkusAugust/streamlord/blob/master/CLA.md). You keep
the copyright in what you write. The agreement gives me the right to license your contribution on
other terms later, so that one contribution does not lock the project's future. Everything already
published under MIT stays MIT whatever happens.

## Forking

Fork it.

If you need something Streamlord does not do, disagree with a decision, or want to take it
somewhere I will not: take your copy and go. No permission, no explanation.

That you can continue without me is not a weakness in the project. It is the whole point of
giving it away under MIT. All I ask is that what you build gets its own name.

`streamlord-html-pro` contains no Pro code at all, only the attribute names, and is inert without
the bundle you licensed yourself. [That page](/datastar-pro/) explains it in full.
